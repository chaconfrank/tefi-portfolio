By using both sources side by side, we discovered we were giving nearly equal weight to two kinds of content that didn't hold the same value for the professional.

Since the AI already had the interview and analysis information, we proposed adding a second layer of information: an AI-generated summary that would serve as a starting point, which each professional could review and adapt.

We weren't trying to reproduce each center's particular format. The goal was to find a common structure that could be useful across different care contexts.

The question then shifted from only what information to summarize to also deciding how to present it so it would actually be easy to consult.

We took both proposals to a first test with professionals to compare which type of information was most useful.

The test helped us move toward a more direct, organized structure, closer to the type of information professionals needed to consult and later adapt.

But it also let us detect a different problem.

In that first proposal, Analysis and Summary already appeared as the two main outputs, while the transcript remained available as supporting information. However, the Summary tab stayed inactive until the professional approved the Analysis.

</CaseSubsection>

<CaseSubsection compacta titulo="A visible tab wasn't enough if it looked inaccessible" composicion="apilada">

During the test we observed that professionals didn't try to explore a tab they perceived as disabled.

The intention was to show that a step came after the Analysis, but the inactive state achieved the opposite: the Summary was present in the interface, but it didn't help convey what would happen after approving.

This led us to change the interaction: Analysis and Summary needed to stay visible and accessible, even when the Summary hadn't been generated yet.

This first validation therefore produced two decisions that would shape the next version: moving forward with a more structured, direct Summary, and keeping both sections accessible to make the path between Analysis and Summary understandable.

</CaseSubsection>

<CaseSection
  compacta
  id="flujo-completo"
  numero="04"
  etiqueta="Designing the complete flow"
  titulo="The AI generates. The professional validates."
  composicion="apilada"
  destacado="We prioritized the quality of information over immediacy: AI speeds up the work, but professional judgment remains part of the process."
>

Adding the Summary raised an important decision: at what point should it be generated, and from which information?

Generating it directly from the interview would have been more immediate, but it could also carry AI errors from one output to the next. That's why we decided to keep an intermediate validation step.

The professional had to review, edit, and approve the Analysis first. Only then would the AI use that already-validated information to generate the Summary.

/>

<CaseSubsection compacta titulo="Two outputs with different functions" composicion="apilada">

With the new architecture, Analysis and Summary moved to the first level of Life Story, while the transcript remained available as supporting information.

The Analysis kept the complete content organized by life stages and was the space where the professional could review and correct the AI's work.

The Summary offered a shorter, more structured version, designed to quickly locate the most relevant information.

After the first validation, both sections remained visible and accessible, making it more evident that they were part of the same process.

</CaseSubsection>

With the new flow already integrated into Amaia, we ran 5 usability sessions with professionals from five different centers, represented here as Center A, B, C, D, and E to preserve their identity.

To analyze the results, I compared the observations from the five sessions against the hypotheses I had previously defined about the happy path.

The tests also revealed more isolated issues: confusion about what Regenerate did, low visibility of some editing tools, or differences in how certain flow elements were used.

<CaseSubsection
  compacta
  titulo="What we validated"
  composicion="apilada"
  destacado="The value of the Summary was understood once reached. The main friction was in making the path to it clearer."

Despite the friction along the way, the sessions reinforced two fundamental decisions.

The first was the value of the Summary. When professionals reached it, they understood its role as a shorter version of the Life Story and found it useful to be able to consult and adapt it.

The second was keeping the professional within the generation process. During the tests we observed how they reviewed, corrected, and adapted the AI-generated content before approving it, confirming that professional validation fit how they actually worked.

</CaseSubsection>

To reduce that uncertainty we worked on specific states to communicate what was happening while the information was being processed.

<CaseSection
  compacta
  id="resultado"
  numero="06"
  etiqueta="Result"
  titulo="From generating a report to supporting care"
  composicion="apilada"
>

Life Story evolved from a process focused mainly on generating a report into a flow where AI and professional judgment each play different roles.

The AI processes the interview and structures the information; the professional reviews, corrects, and validates the content; and the Summary turns that work into a shorter version that can later be consulted from Amaia Profesionales and Amaia Conecta.

The goal wasn't to replace professional judgment, but to reduce the work needed to turn a long interview into information that could be reused in daily care.

The value wasn't just in shortening information. The Summary allowed what was learned during an interview to also remain available to other professionals who later cared for that person.
